# Accessible Presentation Delivery Guide

A concise checklist for delivering accessible presentations, distilled from the [UW IT accessibility guidelines](https://it.wisc.edu/learn/make-it-accessible/accessible-online-and-in-person-presentations/) and UW Policy UW-519.

The skill surfaces a brief version of this guide to the user after generating a presentation so the presenter knows how to deliver it accessibly.

## Before the Presentation

- **Share materials ahead of time** when possible, so attendees can review at their own pace or with assistive technology
- **Confirm accommodations** with event organizers (live captions, sign language interpreting, translation services)
- **Test the microphone** even in small rooms - audio matters for everyone, not just people with hearing loss
- **Check the venue** for accessibility (entrances, seating, sightlines)

## During the Presentation

- **Use a microphone** every time, even when the room "feels small"
- **Speak slower than natural conversation pace** to support comprehension and any live transcription
- **Describe visuals on each slide aloud**, not just what's projected:
  - Charts: "This bar chart shows enrollment grew from 5,000 to 7,500 between 2019 and 2024"
  - Images: "This is a photo of the Memorial Union terrace at sunset"
  - Diagrams: "The diagram shows three agents - Research, Model, and Review - connected in a pipeline"
- **Repeat audience questions** before answering, so everyone hears the question
- **Require microphone use** during Q&A; ask participants to state their name when speaking
- **Describe visual room information** (e.g., "About a third of the audience just raised their hands")

## Online-Specific

- **Avoid virtual backgrounds** - they reduce visual clarity for low-vision viewers and hurt low-bandwidth connections
- **Enable live captions** if the platform supports them (Zoom, Teams, Google Meet)
- **Pin the speaker view** when sign language interpretation is happening
- **Mute when not speaking** to keep the audio channel clear

## After the Presentation

- **Share the slide file** with the audience (the generated HTML works as a standalone file)
- **Provide a feedback channel** for accessibility concerns (email or form link)
- **Share recording or transcripts** when available
